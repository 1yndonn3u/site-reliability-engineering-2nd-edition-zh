Preface

前言

Ten years ago, the first edition of Site Reliability Engineering set out to explain what Site Reliability Engineering (SRE) is and how Site Reliability Engineers (SREs) do it at Google. In the subsequent years, the role has become widespread beyond Google, and the approach we described has become broadly accepted in the industry. Meanwhile, the technology underpinning running large-scale distributed computing systems has become much more standardized and commoditized: unless you have very specific needs, you’re unlikely to build your own load balancer or your own distributed consensus system with Paxos—you’re probably either buying it as a service from a cloud provider or running an instance of open source software.

十年前，Site Reliability Engineering 第 1 版着手阐释什么是站点可靠性工程（SRE），以及 Google 的站点可靠性工程师（SRE）是如何践行它的。在随后的岁月里，这一角色已在 Google 之外广为普及，我们所描述的方法也被业界广泛接受。与此同时，支撑大规模分布式计算系统运行的技术，已经高度标准化，成了现成的通用件：除非你有非常特殊的需求，否则你多半不会去自建负载均衡器，也不会用 Paxos 自建分布式共识系统——你很可能要么直接买云厂商的托管服务，要么自己跑一套开源软件。

Furthermore, as we write this, AI is in the process of radically accelerating change in our field, as well as many others. We have no crystal ball to forecast what the future of running production environments will be like in another 10 years—or even, quite possibly, 10 months.1

而且就在我们写这本书的时候，AI 正让我们这个行业（以及其他许多行业）的变化大大提速。我们没有水晶球，没法预言再过 10 年，甚至很可能只要再过 10 个月，运维生产环境会变成什么样子。1

But what hasn’t changed? The fundamentals: SREs continue to be engineers, focusing on system reliability, for large-scale distributed computing services.

那什么没变？最根本的东西没变：SRE 仍然是工程师，关注的仍然是大规模分布式计算服务的系统可靠性。

All of those trends, combined with what we know has stayed the same, have caused us to shift the focus of this edition toward the things we didn’t address in much detail the first time around—things that support well-functioning teams: the sociotechnical aspects of SRE. As a result, you’ll see more on understanding the value of what we do and how to provide that value and represent it within an organization—which was important 10 years ago but is even more important now. Something we also learned since the first edition is just how widespread this work—managing the whole lifecycle of software—is, and by how many different names it is called. We wrote in The Site Reliability Workbook about the relationship between DevOps and SRE, but platform engineering has emerged since, and staff engineers often have that combination of deep expertise and holistic responsibility that is the hallmark of SRE. Regardless of what the role is called, though, we hope the material in the book will be of relevance to those who care about production.

这些变化，加上我们知道一直没变的那些东西，让我们把这一版的重心放到了第 1 版没怎么展开的内容上，也就是让团队运转良好的那些因素：SRE 的社会技术层面。所以这一版会多讲几件事：怎么理解我们工作的价值，怎么在组织里创造这份价值，又怎么让组织看到它。这件事 10 年前就很重要，现在更重要了。第 1 版出版后我们还发现，这类工作（管理软件的整个生命周期）其实到处都有，叫法也五花八门。我们在 The Site Reliability Workbook 里讨论过 DevOps 与 SRE 的关系；但那之后又出现了平台工程，而资深工程师（staff engineer）往往既有很深的专业功底，又要对全局负责，这正是 SRE 的典型特征。不过，不管这个角色叫什么，我们都希望本书对所有关心生产环境的人有用。

In the first edition, we used the metaphor of the work before and after the birth of a system being akin to labor and parenting. We believe this analogy still holds, especially in our current situation (see Chapter 19), where birthing a system has gotten easier but it’s not yet clear that parenting or housework has. So much of software engineering is the question of what you can successfully ignore, and the fact that there is a whole lifecycle cannot be ignored.

在第 1 版中，我们用了一个比喻：把系统诞生前后的工作，比作分娩与育儿。我们相信这一类比如今依然成立，尤其是在我们当下的处境中（见第 19 章）：如今让系统“出生”容易了，但“养育”和“家务”有没有跟着变容易，还很难说。软件工程很大程度上就是判断哪些东西可以放心不管；而系统确实有一整套生命周期这件事，恰恰不能不管。

However, another concept from the software engineering discourse has gained prominence in the decade since our first edition: technical debt. This is the term we use to capture the uneasy scenario we arrive at unintentionally when we have built the software on top of itself too much, and everything becomes difficult. Abstractions are stretched to their breaking point; pushing to production feels like trying to hammer a cube through a round hole, where the hammer occasionally bounces off and hits you in the knee. Eventually, you just want to stop and break apart the blocks of what you’ve built.

不过，第 1 版出版后的这十年里，软件工程圈还有一个概念越来越受关注：技术债。我们用这个词来形容一种让人头疼的局面：谁也没打算走到这一步，但软件在旧代码上一层层堆得太多，结果什么都变得很难做。各种抽象被撑到了极限；往生产环境推代码，就像硬要把方块锤进圆孔，锤子还时不时弹回来砸到你的膝盖。到最后，你只想停下来，把搭好的积木拆掉重来。

We draw a comparison here to technical debt because, despite its ubiquity in software systems, it is often dismissed as only a source of developer sadness. But for those of us who care about production, technical debt is a leading indicator of something important: unreliability.

我们之所以在这里拿技术债来比较，是因为技术债在软件系统里随处可见，却常被看轻，好像它只不过是让开发者心烦的东西。但在我们这些关心生产环境的人看来，技术债是一个重要问题的先行指标，这个问题就是：不可靠。

In the financial world, debt is not inherently bad. It is a leverage tool. You borrow capital to accelerate growth and invest in the future, accepting that you will pay it back with interest. In software, we do the same. We borrow from our future stability to ship a feature today, trading perhaps a robust architecture or the comprehensive testing that would have required more time and effort for speed to market.

在金融领域，负债本身并不是坏事，它是一种杠杆。你借钱来加快增长、投资未来，也接受以后要连本带息地还。做软件也是一样。我们向未来的稳定性借债，好让一个功能今天就能上线：为了更快推向市场，可能放弃了更稳健的架构，或者省掉了更全面的测试，因为这些都要多花时间和精力。

But software debt, like financial debt, always demands repayment, with interest.

但软件欠下的债和金融债一样，迟早要连本带息地还。

In production, that interest is paid in complexity, friction, and fragile systems. Every shortcut shrinks our margin for error. If we ignore the debt, the interest compounds silently. Eventually, if left unchecked, we face reliability bankruptcy. This isn’t just a slowdown in feature delivery; it is the outage that destroys user trust, or the codebase that’s so brittle that no one wants to work on it, much less deploy it.

在生产环境里，我们要为这笔利息付出代价：系统更复杂，做什么都更费劲，整个系统也更脆弱。每走一次捷径，我们的容错空间就小一点。如果一直不管这笔债，利息就会在不知不觉中越滚越多。任由它发展下去，最后就会落得“可靠性破产”。到那一步，问题就不只是新功能上线变慢了，而是会出现毁掉用户信任的大故障，或者代码库脆弱到没人愿意碰，更别说部署上线了。

SRE is not a crusade for zero technical debt—a state that is both economically foolish and practically impossible for any system that is actually useful. Instead, SRE is the discipline of managing this risk portfolio. We are both engineers and production advisors, helping the organization decide when to borrow safely and when we must pay down the principal according to our agreed-upon risk ceilings, temporarily halting feature development to invest in repayment.

SRE 并不是要搞一场消灭技术债的运动。对任何真正有用的系统来说，追求零技术债既不划算，实际上也做不到。SRE 要做的，是管好这一组风险。我们既是工程师，也是生产环境的顾问，帮组织判断什么时候可以放心借债，什么时候必须按照大家事先商定的风险上限偿还本金，哪怕这意味着暂停功能开发，把精力投到还债上。

This edition is about how we manage that balance. Many of the systems we run and the approaches we take have changed in the 10 years since the first edition, but this hasn’t: reliability is not a static state to be achieved and forgotten. It is an ongoing negotiation between complexity and business needs. The chapters that follow explore the tools, culture, and practices we use at Google to successfully navigate that complexity to provide reliable services.

这一版要讲的，就是我们如何把握这种平衡。第 1 版出版后的 10 年里，我们运维的很多系统、采用的很多方法都变了，但有一点没变：可靠性不是做到一次就能撂下不管的固定状态，而是要在复杂度和业务需求之间不断权衡、反复协商。接下来的各章会介绍我们在 Google 用到的工具、文化和实践，正是靠这些，我们才驾驭住了复杂度，提供了可靠的服务。

How to Read This Book

如何阅读本书

Like the first edition, this book is a series of essays written by members and alumni of Google’s Site Reliability Engineering organization. This time, we have expanded our view with authors from our Google partner teams who work with Google Cloud customers, and even to case studies from Disney, Etsy, Microsoft, and Spotify. It’s much more like conference proceedings than it is like a standard book by a single author or a small number of authors. Each chapter is intended to be read as part of a coherent whole, but a good deal can be gained by focusing on whatever subject particularly interests you. Where other chapters or sections support or inform the text, we reference them so you can explore accordingly.

和第 1 版一样，这本书是一组文章的合集，作者是 Google 站点可靠性工程组织的在职和离职成员。这一次我们把视野放得更宽，请来了 Google 合作伙伴团队的作者（他们和 Google Cloud 的客户一起工作），还收录了 Disney、Etsy、Microsoft 和 Spotify 的案例。所以，比起一位或几位作者写的普通图书，它更像一本会议论文集。各章合起来是一个完整的整体，但只挑你特别感兴趣的主题来读，也会有不少收获。如果别的章节能支撑或补充正文内容，我们会标出引用，方便你顺着读下去。

While the first edition of this book covered the foundational principles and practices of SRE, this edition delves into how these concepts and practices have developed and matured over the past decade. Additionally, we tackle some new topics: STPA and CAST (Chapter 12), and how AI is changing the SRE profession (Chapters 19, 20, and 22). We chose not to spend much time on topics that are evergreen and were well covered in the previous edition. As a result, you’ll see that the chapters don’t map one to one from the first edition to this one. It’s helpful to think of this work as a supplement to that book, rather than a wholesale replacement.

本书第 1 版讲的是 SRE 的基础原则和实践；这一版则深入讨论，这些理念和实践在过去十年里是怎样发展成熟的。另外，我们还加了一些新主题：STPA 和 CAST（第 12 章），以及 AI 正在怎样改变 SRE 这个职业（第 19、20 和 22 章）。有些主题一直不过时，上一版也已经讲得很充分，这次就不再多花篇幅了。所以你会发现，这一版的章节和第 1 版并不是一一对应的。最好把这本书看作第 1 版的补充，而不是全盘替代。

You don’t need to read the book in any particular order, though we’d suggest at least starting with Chapters 1, 2, and 4. Reading cover to cover is, of course, also useful and possible. The chapters are grouped thematically into six parts: Part I, “SRE in Context”; Part II, “Sociotechnical Foundations”; Part III, “Situational Awareness and Continuous Preparedness”; Part IV, “Infrastructure Design”; Part V, “Resilient Products and Emergent Reliability”; and Part VI, “Outlook”. Each part has a small introduction that highlights what the individual chapters are about.

这本书不必按固定顺序读，不过我们建议至少先读第 1、2 和 4 章。当然，从头读到尾也完全可以，同样有收获。全书按主题分成六个部分：第一部分《情境中的 SRE》；第二部分《社会技术基础》；第三部分《态势感知与持续备战》；第四部分《基础设施设计》；第五部分《有韧性的产品与涌现式可靠性》；以及第六部分《展望》。每个部分开头都有一小段导读，简单介绍其中各章讲什么。

We hope this book will be at least as useful and interesting to you as putting it together was for us.

希望你读这本书的收获和乐趣，至少不亚于我们编写它时的体会。

— The Editors
